## MEDDPICC Framework

MEDDPICC is the standard qualification framework for complex B2B enterprise
sales. Each letter is a dimension of deal health. A deal missing any
dimension has a gap to close.

### M — Metrics

**What it means:** Quantified business impact. What are the measurable
outcomes the buyer expects from this purchase?

**Questions that surface it:**
- "What does success look like in 6 months? How would you measure it?"
- "What's the cost of NOT solving this problem this year?"
- "What metrics does your team report to leadership on this area?"

**Scoring rubric:**
- **2 (Strong):** Specific metrics documented — "Reduce time-to-onboard
  from 4 weeks to 1 week" or "Recover 15 hours/week of engineering time."
  Buyer has connected these to business impact (revenue, cost, risk).
- **1 (Partial):** General benefit described but not quantified — "Speed up
  the process" or "Reduce manual work."
- **0 (Weak/Unknown):** No measurable outcome has been articulated. Value
  is being assumed.

**Red flag:** If the buyer cannot articulate a measurable outcome, they
likely haven't secured internal budget approval and are still exploring.

---

### E — Economic Buyer

**What it means:** The person who can write the check — or approve the
budget transfer — without asking anyone else. Not the champion. Often
one level up from the champion.

**Questions that surface it:**
- "Who owns the budget for this initiative?"
- "Who would sign off on a purchase like this?"
- "Is there anyone else who needs to be involved in the final decision?"

**Scoring rubric:**
- **2 (Strong):** Economic Buyer identified by name and title. Rep has met
  with them or champion has confirmed they are engaged.
- **1 (Partial):** EB identified by title but not yet engaged. Champion
  has confirmed the right person but no meeting scheduled.
- **0 (Weak/Unknown):** EB unknown. Rep is working only with users or
  middle management.

**Red flag:** A deal in Proposal stage without EB engagement is at extreme
risk of a surprise "no budget" at the end.

---

### D — Decision Criteria

**What it means:** The specific criteria the buyer will use to choose a
vendor. What does the evaluation scorecard look like?

**Questions that surface it:**
- "What criteria will you use to evaluate vendors?"
- "What does the perfect solution look like to you?"
- "Are there things that would immediately disqualify a vendor?"

**Scoring rubric:**
- **2 (Strong):** Decision criteria documented. Rep knows the weighting
  (what matters most). Has influenced the criteria in Hero's favor.
- **1 (Partial):** General criteria known (e.g., "ease of use, security,
  price") but not specific or weighted.
- **0 (Weak/Unknown):** Criteria unknown. Buyer is evaluating on unknown
  terms.

**Red flag:** If you haven't influenced the decision criteria, a competitor
probably has.

---

### D — Decision Process

**What it means:** How the decision will actually be made. Who's involved,
what steps are required, what the timeline looks like, and what approvals
are needed.

**Questions that surface it:**
- "Walk me through how a decision like this typically gets made here."
- "Who else needs to be involved before you can move forward?"
- "What does legal/security/procurement review look like for a deal like this?"
- "What would need to happen for you to move forward by [target date]?"

**Scoring rubric:**
- **2 (Strong):** Decision process mapped step by step with owners and
  timeline. Rep has done procurement and legal navigation before at this
  company (or champion has confirmed the process).
- **1 (Partial):** High-level process known ("we need IT and legal to
  review") but steps and timeline not mapped.
- **0 (Weak/Unknown):** Process unknown. "I'll let you know when we're
  ready to move forward" — rep is not in control.

---

### I — Identify Pain

**What it means:** The specific business pain driving this purchase.
Not features they want — the problem that's costing them money, time,
risk, or competitive disadvantage today.

**Questions that surface it:**
- "What's the biggest challenge you're facing in this area right now?"
- "What happens if you don't solve this in the next 6 months?"
- "How is this problem affecting the broader business?"
- "How long has this been a problem? Why is now the right time to fix it?"

**Scoring rubric:**
- **2 (Strong):** Specific pain articulated with business impact. "Our
  onboarding process takes 4 weeks, which delays revenue recognition and
  causes early churn." Pain is connected to executive-level priorities.
- **1 (Partial):** Pain described but not quantified or connected to
  business impact. "Our process is slow."
- **0 (Weak/Unknown):** Pain is tactical or unclear. Buying because
  "we wanted to evaluate options" — no urgency.

---

### C — Champion

**What it means:** An internal advocate who believes in your solution,
has credibility with the Economic Buyer, and is willing to sell internally
on your behalf when you're not in the room.

**Signs of a true champion (not just a friendly contact):**
- Has shared internal information you shouldn't otherwise have access to
- Has actively advocated for you in internal meetings
- Has arranged access to decision-makers for you
- Has a stake in this purchase succeeding

**Scoring rubric:**
- **2 (Strong):** Named champion who has done at least 2 champion behaviors
  (see above). Has access to and credibility with the EB.
- **1 (Partial):** Friendly contact who likes the solution but hasn't
  shown champion behaviors yet. Or: champion lacks access to EB.
- **0 (Weak/Unknown):** No internal advocate. Everyone met is just evaluating.

**Red flag:** If your champion doesn't have access to the EB, or lacks
credibility, you need to find a better champion or help yours build
credibility. A champion without influence cannot move the deal.

---

### C — Competition

**What it means:** Who else is in the evaluation, how they're positioned,
and what your competitive posture is.

**Questions that surface it:**
- "Are you evaluating other vendors?"
- "Have you seen [competitor] in this space?"
- "What would a vendor need to do to win this evaluation?"

**Scoring rubric:**
- **2 (Strong):** Competitive landscape fully known. Know who's in it,
  where we stand, and have a specific strategy for each competitor.
- **1 (Partial):** Some competitors known but not all. General competitive
  awareness but no specific counter-strategy.
- **0 (Weak/Unknown):** "We're not evaluating anyone else" — likely not
  true and not healthy. Or: competitive situation completely unknown.

## MEDDPICC Scoring

| Dimension | Score |
|---|---|
| Metrics | 0–2 |
| Economic Buyer | 0–2 |
| Decision Criteria | 0–2 |
| Decision Process | 0–2 |
| Identify Pain | 0–2 |
| Champion | 0–2 |
| Competition | 0–2 |
| **Total** | **0–14** |

**Normalize:** `(total / 14) × 100 = meddpicc_score`

**Interpretation:**
- 80–100 — Strongly qualified. High confidence in close.
- 60–79 — Well qualified with minor gaps. Close plan viable.
- 40–59 — Moderate qualification. Significant gaps to close before advancing.
- 20–39 — Poorly qualified. Do not advance to Proposal without major improvement.
- 0–19 — Unqualified. Recommend qualify-out review.

## Red Flag Checklist

Any single red flag is worth noting. Three or more flags = mandatory
qualification review with manager.

- [ ] No Economic Buyer identified (name and title unknown)
- [ ] No Economic Buyer access (identified but never met)
- [ ] No Champion (only user-level contacts)
- [ ] Champion has no access or credibility with EB
- [ ] No compelling event — no "why now" documented
- [ ] Timeline is undefined or "end of year" with no urgency signal
- [ ] Budget not confirmed; no approval path identified
- [ ] Decision process unknown — "I'll let you know"
- [ ] Decision criteria unknown — evaluating on unclear terms
- [ ] Rep is single-threaded (one contact only)
- [ ] Pain is only tactical (no business impact articulated)
- [ ] Competitive situation unknown
- [ ] Champion is leaving or role changed
- [ ] Deal has stalled in the same stage past its stale threshold (see `pipeline-management`)
- [ ] Close date has pushed twice or more

---

## Confirmed vs. Assumed

The most important discipline in qualification. For each dimension, note:

- **Confirmed:** The rep was told this directly by the buyer. It's in notes.
- **Inferred:** The rep concluded this from indirect signals or reasonable
  assumption.
- **Unknown:** No information. The rep is guessing.

When scoring, only award full points for **Confirmed** information.
**Inferred** information caps at 1. **Unknown** is always 0.

---

## Qualify-out guidance

Recommend qualify-out review when:
- MEDDPICC score < 25 after 3 qualification conversations — canonical thresholds: `pipeline-management`
- No EB identified at Proposal stage
- Champion departed or gone silent with no replacement
- Compelling event evaporated (the "why now" no longer exists)
- Deal has stalled in the same stage past its stale threshold (see `pipeline-management`)

Qualify-out is not a failure — it's pipeline hygiene. A deal that can't
close wastes rep time, pollutes the forecast, and creates false confidence
for the team.
